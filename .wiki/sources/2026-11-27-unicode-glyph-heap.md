# Photograph a stroke made entirely of type

The creator said that the previous clip looked like a transition and that its small characters resembled a neat molecular array. For this shot, the editor removed the original ink from the background and set the whole stroke on a single lossless plate. It contains 19,699 impressions from 72 Unicode characters. Four irregular passes vary position, size, angle, overlap and ink value. The original contour guides placement. The three Han forms come from the first-edition scans. The additional forms are an editorial composition. [Original mapping row, page 171](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode1.0.0/HanCharts2.pdf#page=3) · [Two forms, page 98](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode1.0.0/HanCharts1.pdf#page=76)

The camera uses the same 7424 by 2048 plate from the first frame to the last. The previous glyph grid and opacity changes in [[2026-11-27-unicode-glyph-zoom]] have been superseded. In a comparison using two identical canvas surfaces, erasing a fixed 0.32 by 0.32 scan-pixel patch changed 8 image pixels in the opening, 161 at an intermediate magnification and 16,281 near the top of the move. At five matching magnifications the inward and outward views had identical pixels. The revised voice describes the overlapping letters.
