# Korean contributors in Unicode 1.0 and distinguishing John C. Dvorak

While the main text of *The Unicode Standard, Version 1.0, Volume 2* (p. 12) attributes the February 1990 Seoul proposal for a joint Han study group only to “the Korean delegation,” the front matter of Volumes 1 and 2, the 1990 `X3L2` register, and Korean institutional records name the individuals involved. See [[2026-11-27-unicode-source-chronology-and-people]] and the [Research companion](../../episodes/unicode-1.0/chronology.ko.md).

## Korean contributors documented in primary records

- **Oh Young-taik (오영택, Korean Bureau of Standards / 공업진흥청 표준국 과장)** — Named in [Volume 2 Acknowledgments](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode1.0.0/V2Acknowledge.pdf) (p. v) among the five main CJK-JRG delegation leaders alongside Miyazawa Akira, Zhang Zhoucai, Su Liang, and Dominic Cheng. Also recorded in the [National Institute of Korean Language chronology (`6. 한자 표준화`)](https://www.korean.go.kr/nkview/history/h_13.html) attending the 5th CJK-JRG meeting in Hawaii (Nov 30 – Dec 4, 1992) as part of a delegation led by Kongju National University Professor Hyun Kyu-seob (현규섭), with Im Won-seon (임원선, Ministry of Culture), Seo Ji-won (서지원), Kim Chul (김철, IBM Korea), and Cho Nam-ho (조남호, NIKL).
- **Lee Choon Tack (이춘택, Kongju National University / 공주대학교 사범대학 문헌정보교육과 명예교수)** — Named in [Volume 2 Acknowledgments](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode1.0.0/V2Acknowledge.pdf) (pp. v–vi) as the Korean member of the five-person CJK-JRG editorial subcommittee responsible for final review and verification of the *CJK Ideographic Character Unified Repertoire and Ordering* (alongside Lee Collins, Kao Tien-Cheu, Koike Tateo, and Zhang Zhoucai). [Kongju National University’s official faculty profile](https://lise.kongju.ac.kr/ZA0370/9944/subview.do) records him as professor (1986–Feb 2008), `1990년 ~ 1991년: CJK-JRG 한국 편집위원`, and Professor Emeritus specializing in cataloging and Hanja character codes.
- **Kyongsok Kim (김경석, University of North Dakota / State University of North Dakota → Pusan National University / 부산대학교 정보컴퓨터공학부 명예교수)** — Named in both [Volume 1 Acknowledgments](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode1.0.0/Acknowledge.pdf) (pp. v–vi, listed at University of North Dakota) and [Volume 2 Acknowledgments](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode1.0.0/V2Acknowledge.pdf) (p. vi, listed at State University of North Dakota) for significant contributions to creating or reviewing Version 1.0. Returning to Pusan National University, he chaired the Korean Bureau of Standards character code committee (`Korea JTC1/SC2`) and represented Korea at `ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 2/WG 2` from 1992 ([Electronic Times report, 1995-04-13](https://www.etnews.com/199504130047)), championing the *Cheot-ga-kkeut* (`첫가끝`) Hangul Jamo encoding beyond Volume 1 p. 108’s 2,350 `KS C 5601` syllables (`U+3400`–`U+3D2D`, [ch03_3.pdf p. 11](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode1.0.0/ch03_3.pdf#page=11)) toward modern 11,172 Hangul syllables and publishing 『컴퓨터 속의 한글 이야기』 (1995). He is listed today as Professor Emeritus on [PNU Scholar](https://scholar.pusan.ac.kr/researchers/1103/).
- **Jeoung Sung Cho / J.S. Cho (조정성)** — Recorded in the [1990 ANSI `X3L2` Document Register](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L1990/Register-1990.html) as author of `X3L2/90-19K` (*Information about the [WG2] ad hoc meeting in Seoul [26–28 February 1990]*, Dec 22, 1989) and recipient of WG2 convener Mike Ksar’s letter `X3L2/90-131` (*Letter, M. Ksar to J.S. Cho, re Korean Position on 10646 change*, Sept 17, 1990).
- **Hye-young Im (임혜영), Jae Chong (정재, Hahn Computer Institute), and Haesung Kim (김해성, Apple)** — Named in both [Volume 1 Acknowledgments](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode1.0.0/Acknowledge.pdf) (p. v) and [Volume 2 Acknowledgments](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode1.0.0/V2Acknowledge.pdf) (p. vi): Im and Chong within the University of Toronto East Asian Studies Unicode CJK Unification Verification Project led by Prof. Kazuko Nakajima, and Haesung Kim at Apple among Version 1.0 contributors and reviewers.

No commercial-use-cleared portraits have been verified yet for these Korean contributors; per editorial guidance, keep verified names and sources without unverified photos.

## John C. Dvorak vs. August Dvorak

The `press` scene (`1992.09 · PC Magazine`) in the film cites **John C. Dvorak** (April 5, 1946 – July 20, 2026), an American technology columnist who wrote [“Kiss Your ASCII Goodbye”](https://www.unicode.org/announcements/kissascii.pdf) in *PC Magazine* (September 15, 1992, p. 93; [obituary in *The Hollywood Reporter*, July 27, 2026](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/john-dvorak-dead-tech-journalist-no-agenda-inside-track-1236657317/)).

He is **not** the creator of the Dvorak keyboard layout. The **Dvorak Simplified Keyboard** was co-developed in the 1930s (*Typewriting Behavior*, 1936) by **Dr. August Dvorak** (May 5, 1894 – October 9, 1975), Professor of Education at the University of Washington, together with William Dealey—eleven years before the 1986 Xerox Han mapping work began. Biographical profiles and interviews commonly describe August Dvorak as John C. Dvorak’s uncle/relative (sharing the Americanized pronunciation `/ˈdvɔːræk/`), though primary genealogical proof rests on secondary accounts.
