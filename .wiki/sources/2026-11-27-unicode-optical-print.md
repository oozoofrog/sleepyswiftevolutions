# Let the scan resolve into weathered type

The creator clarified that a convincing optical transition matters more than drawing microtype in the opening frame. They also asked for the enlarged characters to have the noise of the original print. This revises the literal single-plate approach in [[2026-11-27-unicode-glyph-heap]]. The irregular pile remains the subject of the close view.

The small view uses the decoded mapping row. As the camera approaches the right tip, fine variations in the letter plate enter first along the gray edge. Its average ink tone takes over later in the darker stroke. The return retraces the same magnifications. The new small glyph impressions sample edge irregularity and ink-density variation from the original chart. Added faces are rasterized at approximately the sampling pitch of its printed characters. The paper and ink marks stay fixed throughout the shot. [Mapping row, printed page 171](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode1.0.0/HanCharts2.pdf#page=3) · [Han character samples, printed page 98](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode1.0.0/HanCharts1.pdf#page=76)

The uncompressed picture window matched a scan-only rendering pixel for pixel at 1×, 2× and 4×. At 8× the mean absolute difference was 0.65 gray levels across that window. The camera reaches 128×. Five equal-magnification samples matched on the inward and outward moves. The shot contains 312 frames with narration.
