# Open with Becker's dated proposal and one physical glyph plate

The creator asked for a dated human action as the first line, and for the first 一 itself to consist of tiny Unicode glyphs. The cover of Unicode 88 names Joseph D. Becker and gives August 29, 1988. Section 1.1 describes a draft encoding design and reserves individual character assignments for later work. The consortium chronology records earlier joint work at Xerox and Apple in 1986–1987. The film opens with the proposal, identifies U+4E00 as the present-day number, and travels back to the earlier work. [Unicode 88, cover and abstract](https://www.unicode.org/history/Unicode88.pdf) · [Chronology of Unicode 1.0](https://www.unicode.org/history/versionone.html)

The film uses the modern glyph as a stencil. The visible ink comes from 38,790 overlapping impressions of mixed sizes. The first frame, all subsequent zoom levels and the Xerox cut sample the same bitmap. Removing a small region changes each of four camera samples in both aspect ratios. Both raw edit boundaries still match.
