# Read the chronology before selecting scenes

The creator asked for the original chronology, its participants and the circumstances behind each story. The English source runs from the Xerox team in 1986–1987 to Dvorak’s magazine article in September 1992. The Korean research companion maps all 48 date headings in the creator’s article to six stories. It preserves the source discrepancy for the Seoul meeting and identifies the dated records used for the epilogue. [English chronology](https://www.unicode.org/history/versionone.html) · [Creator’s article](https://oozoofrog.dev/blog/chronology_unicode/) · [Research companion](../../episodes/unicode-1.0/chronology.ko.md)

The opening distinguishes the Xerox mapping of Japanese and Chinese characters from Apple’s July 1988 acquisition of RLG’s CJK character database. The library setting comes from Karen Smith-Yoshimura’s own account. The last exhibit follows Lee Collins from Xerox to the 2026 Vietnamese proposal he co-authored with Eiso Chan and Ngô Trung Việt. The proposal reproduces the documentary evidence used to question a glyph. [Library account](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/jeal/vol1993/iss101/12/) · [Original 2026 proposal, page 1](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2026/26068-v-source-character-u2b8a0.pdf)

The closing chapter also returns to Xerox participant Nelson Ng and Japanese standards contributor Akira Miyazawa. The museum lists Ng as its chair. NII lists Miyazawa as professor emeritus. Images of Becker and Davis are credited to the Consortium’s historical album and carry an unknown capture date. [Palo Alto Museum board](https://paloaltomuseum.org/team-member/nelson-ng/) · [NII emeritus faculty](https://www.nii.ac.jp/en/faculty/emeritus/) · [Unicode photo album](https://www.unicode.org/history/photoalbum/)
